Laue lenses

Focusing γ rays for astrophysics

Above 100 keV no mirror focuses photons. Bent crystals, arranged in a lens, can.

The physics.

The sky between about 100 keV and 1 MeV is still poorly ex­plored, al­though it holds open ques­tions such as the origin of the 511 keV po­si­tron-annihila­tion line from the Ga­lac­tic Centre and the physics of su­per­no­va explo­sions. At these en­er­gies mirrors no longer reflect and refrac­tion is neg­li­gi­ble, so tele­scopes collect photons di­rect­ly on large de­tec­tors, whose back­ground grows with their size. A lens that con­cen­trat­ed the photons on a small de­tec­tor would greatly improve the sen­si­tiv­i­ty; only diffrac­tion in crys­tals can make one.

A crystal re­flects photons of wave­length λ when they meet its planes, of spacing d, at the Bragg angle

2d sin θB = λ

For 100 keV photons on silicon (111) planes θB is about 1.1°. In transmis­sion, or Laue, ge­om­e­try the photons cross the crystal and leave it de­flect­ed by 2θB.

A Laue lens places many crys­tals on con­cen­tric rings, each tilted to dif­fract towards a common focus. Since the deflec­tion is 2θB and θB falls as the energy rises, outer rings focus lower en­er­gies and inner rings higher ones.

The ef­fi­cien­cy depends on the crys­tals. In a mosaic crystal, made of slight­ly mis­aligned blocks, photons are dif­fract­ed back and forth and at most half leave in the dif­fract­ed direc­tion. In a crystal with curved diffract­ing planes the Bragg condi­tion changes con­tin­u­ous­ly across the thick­ness, so photons are not dif­fract­ed back: the ef­fi­cien­cy can exceed 50%, and the cur­va­ture also con­cen­trates the dif­fract­ed beam into a smaller spot.

We bend silicon and ger­ma­nium plates without holders: stressed thin films, surface grooves, sandblast­ing or carbon-fibre layers curve the plate, and the quasi-mosaic effect, which follows from the an­iso­trop­ic elas­tic­i­ty of the crystal, curves the diffract­ing planes inside it. To fix the crys­tals in the lens we are replac­ing space-qual­i­fied resins with anodic bonding, for higher ef­fi­cien­cy and more ac­cu­rate orienta­tion.

What we have shown.

Our self-stand­ing bent silicon and ger­ma­nium crys­tals reach diffrac­tion ef­fi­cien­cies above 75%, about twice the mosaic crys­tals used before. OTELLO, an ESA con­tract led by the Uni­ver­si­ty of Ferrara with INAF-OAS, INRiM and DTM Tech­nol­o­gies, is build­ing two lens modules for 50–700 keV with every crystal aligned to 30 µrad.

Silicon plates up to 5 mm thick bent to radii down to 30 m by carbon-fibre deposition, with diffraction efficiency close to theory.
