Simulation

Crystal physics in Geant4

Channeling, coherent radiation and pair production in oriented crystals, simulated in Geant4 and in our own codes.

The physics.

Geant4, the toolkit used to simulate detectors and beam lines throughout particle physics, treats matter as amorphous, with atoms placed at random. In a crystal this fails whenever a particle travels close to a plane or an axis, where it feels the averaged, continuous potential of many atoms at once.

We follow each particle in that continuous potential, step by step along its trajectory. Comparing its transverse energy with the depth of the well tells whether it is trapped between planes (channeled), crossing them over the barrier, or reflected by a bent plane; incoherent scattering on nuclei and electrons, which changes the transverse energy, is added along the way.

The radiation of an ultrarelativistic particle on such a curved trajectory is computed with the Baier–Katkov quasi-classical formula, which includes the recoil of the emission; the same approach gives coherent pair production by photons. These models, first written in our codes CRYSTALRAD and RADCHARM++, are now part of Geant4 as G4ChannelingFastSimModel, G4BaierKatkov and G4CoherentPairProduction, so any Geant4 user can simulate oriented crystals in a full setup. Two members of the group belong to the Geant4 Collaboration.
