Oriented calorimeters

Faster electromagnetic showers in PbWO₄

Aligned with the beam, a scintillating crystal contains an electromagnetic shower in a fraction of its usual depth.

The physics.

Electro­magnetic calo­rim­e­ters measure the energy of elec­trons, po­si­trons and photons by absorb­ing their whole shower: the par­ti­cle ra­di­ates, the photons convert into pairs, and the cascade multi­plies until its energy is spent in ionisa­tion and, in a scin­til­la­tor, in light. The depth needed scales with the radia­tion length X0 of the ma­te­ri­al, and ho­mo­ge­neous calo­rim­e­ters of high-Z scintillat­ing crys­tals such as lead tung­state (PbWO4, or PWO) give the best energy resolu­tion.

In a crystal aligned with the beam the picture changes. Along an axis the atoms form strings whose fields add up co­her­ent­ly. For elec­trons, po­si­trons and photons above a few GeV the Lorentz boost raises this field, in the par­ti­cle’s frame, beyond the Schwinger crit­i­cal field of 1.32 × 1016 V/cm. In this strong-field regime of quantum electro­dynamics brems­strah­lung and pair produc­tion are greatly en­hanced: the shower starts earlier and the ef­fec­tive radia­tion length is shorter.

The effect needs align­ment within a few mil­li­ra­di­ans, an ac­cep­tance that depends little on energy, while the shorten­ing of the shower stays pro­nounced at the highest en­er­gies. Hadrons do not feel the orienta­tion, so the dif­fer­ence between electro­magnetic and had­ron­ic showers grows, which helps to iden­ti­fy par­ti­cles.

OREO, the ORi­Ent­ed calO­rim­e­ter, an INFN project led from Ferrara with Legnaro and Milano Bicocca, builds the first calo­rim­e­ter of this kind: 3 × 3 ma­tri­ces of ultra-fast PWO crys­tals read by silicon pho­to­mul­ti­pli­ers, an ori­ent­ed layer in front of a second layer. The axis of every crystal is mea­sured by X-ray diffrac­tion, and the crys­tals are as­sem­bled with inter­ferometric feed­back so that the whole matrix points along the same axis. Uses range from compact calo­rim­e­ters for fixed-target experi­ments and beam dumps for light dark matter to lighter γ-ray tele­scopes in space.

What we have shown.

Align­ing the axes of PbWO₄ crys­tals with the beam makes the electro­magnetic shower develop faster. We built a matrix whose atomic planes are co-aligned within 100 µrad, combin­ing X-ray diffrac­tion, in­ter­fer­om­e­try and preci­sion me­chan­ics. On beam, ori­ent­ed crys­tals gave up to three times more energy deposit, with the effect still visible about one degree off axis: compact calo­rim­e­ters for fixed-target experi­ments and γ-ray satel­lites. CORAL con­tin­ues the line from 2026.
